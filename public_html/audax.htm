What is Audax?

A Truly Unique Cycling Discipline

An Audax (also known as a Randonnée) is a non-competitive cycling event that must be completed within an allocated time limit. It isn't a race, a commercial sportive, or leisurely touring — it is a totally unique, self-reliant adventure.

Riding an Audax is an extraordinary experience that attracts a diverse, friendly, and mutually supportive community. The atmosphere is grounded in camaraderie: everyone is riding against the clock rather than each other, with the focus on completing the distance self-sufficiently before time runs out. Remember — the clock keeps running whenever you stop!

Iconic Challenges

Paris-Brest-Paris & London-Edinburgh-London

Distances in Audax range from friendly 50 km introductory rides up to monumental journeys of more than 1,000 km. Riders generally maintain an average speed between 15 km/h and 30 km/h, balancing riding time with quick café and control stops.

The oldest and most revered of all is the 1,200 km Paris-Brest-Paris (PBP), held every four years in France. PBP traces its origins back to 1891 and was promoted in its early days by Henri Desgrange, who went on to create the Tour de France. To qualify, riders must complete a stringent "Super-Randonneur" series of 200, 300, 400, and 600 km brevets in the months leading up to the event. A number of Cambridge CC riders have proudly completed PBP over the years.

Another crown jewel is the 1,500 km London-Edinburgh-London (LEL). One of our members recently conquered this epic test across Britain.
